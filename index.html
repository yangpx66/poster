ALBA · Assistive bladder actuator

Squeezing a bladder with a folding dome

A cable-driven Hoberman linkage contracts around a silicone bladder phantom to assist voiding. Here is what the simulation does.

The dome closes 5.4× in diameter, but it only reaches the bladder at Ø104. Just 23% of the fold does useful work — so the device should start pre-folded, and the motor only has to drive the last stretch.

Three things the model shows

Fluid is a single equation, not a mesh
Inside the bladder, dynamic pressure is 10⁷× smaller than the static pressure we care about. All the resistance sits at the outlet, so the water becomes one ODE — dV/dt = Qin − Qout(p) — coupled to the deforming wall. That buys the pressure, flow and residual-volume curves for almost nothing.
The phantom checks out against theory
A Neo-Hookean silicone shell, 523.6 mL at rest. Across the inflation range the finite-element pressure tracks the thin-wall analytical solution to within 4.5%, and the agreement improves as the shell thins — which is exactly what the theory predicts it should do.
Drive it by displacement, read the pressure
Cable tension in the linkage is dominated by an uncalibrated joint stiffness, so it can't size a motor on its own. Instead the model sweeps how far the pads press in and reads the pressure that comes back — a curve that can be measured on the bench rig and compared directly.
